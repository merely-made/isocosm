## The sim's schema and saves


### Schema 1. Authored content is native

Authored content enters by assertion, with the record, as the sim's own nouns
filled with authored attributes: a faction is a polity with no members yet,
its name, tags, claims, governance and focus asserted; a place is a site; a
character is a placeless entity, which develops its own body from a selected
recipe and keeps its identity; a law is a rule record; a history line is an
event. Where authored attributes and readings derived from members disagree,
both are kept with their sources and the disagreement shown until an
explicit, admitted change.

Links: draws on 757, 760, 769, 834, 867 · summary Authored content is asserted as native nouns with authored fills; disagreements with derived readings are kept and shown.

*Reviewed 2026-10-22, new: drafted as VTT 2 and placed here at Mark's word
("VTT 2 to the schema"), since any authoring enters this way. The rest of
the area is not yet reviewed.*

### Schema 2. State follows methodology

The sim's nouns are the founding record's five, bodies, space, fields, time
and provenance, each a ladder of scale whose far rung is what the near rung
looks like from far enough away; world state is the fields a place carries.
A thing has its own state exactly when it has its own way of turning a
situation into an action; everything else is a relation, a record or a field
over the things that do. The agent kinds are compositions: a creature; a
faction, a relation acting through its members (one a person defines is a
party); a polity, factions with a collective methodology and so a state; a
lineage, kith and kin. A settlement is a faction until it has a methodology;
a region is terrain. Method goes by tier: reactive creatures,
belief-desire-intention denizens, normative characters; a creature becomes a
denizen when the record keeps it, names it or relates it. State is one ledger
read more coarsely up the levels, the collective providing the coarsening:
body needs matter less as factions and polities grow.

*Not each verified:* the tiers' agent methods.

Links: draws on 8, 9, 37, 38 · summary Own state exactly where there is own method; agent kinds are compositions; method by tier; one ledger read coarser up the levels.

### Schema 3. Provenance and identity

Provenance and identity are two axes. Provenance is born of a lineage, made
by a maker, or intrinsic to the world: constructs are made, the divine
intrinsic, and neither is a rung of identity. Identity runs unnamed, named (a
denizen), sapient (a sophont), and factional. A creature carries its
genotype, a phenotype conditioned by circumstance, and its kingdom; a denizen
adds naming and relationships; a character emerges contingent on its groups
and resolves as both. Anything can become divine, a place or an item too;
once it has, it is there in every fork and branch after its ascent, present
as one omnipresent instance or a cycle of incarnations, and ended only by
prophesied conditions tied to that ascent (how it is earned: Organs 1). An
item can be a sophont: made so, awakened by its story, or inhabited. It acts
through its wielder, by its own powers, or by taking over, and it is never
owned.

*Not yet built:* divinity and sophont items.

### Schema 4. Tenets, alignment and standing

A tenet is the relation that a process yields an effect, with an opinion of
it and the trust placed in it; whether a person may be owned is one, held or
rejected like any other. Every agent's alignment comes from its acts: a
sophont's; a faction's, weighting its members' by their influence; a
polity's, from its decisions. Standing is alignment, reputation (the reach of
one's deeds among those who know of them) and rank a polity asserts.

*Not yet built:* alignment, reputation and standing; every entity holds its
tenets.

Links: draws on 50, 51, 56, 79, 151 · summary A tenet relates a process to its effect with opinion and trust; alignment comes from acts at every rung; standing is alignment, reputation, rank.

*Reviewed 2026-10-22, new. Mark: "Polities apart".*

### Schema 5. Polities

A polity enforces with what it provides and asserts, withholding provision or
revoking rank, office, membership, property, home or borders; force is one
means among any, and its way of deciding suggests its means. Efficacy is read
as a track record, never asserted, and the gap between the constitution and
its members' preference is its legitimacy. A polity has a focus, not a size (a
smiths' guild is one); a contingent one borrows its host's enforcement, so it
is cheap to found, and a host's failure cascades through what rests on it.
Polities under no host are a faction among themselves, a treaty their standing
agreement. Asserting its constitution is a polity's definitive act. Its goals
derive from its alignment, and no will to continue is built in: an institution
seen clinging on is subordinated to its own officers, and an inactive one can
be revived by whoever brings it support.

*Not verified:* enforcement. Polities are built behind the society feature.

### Schema 6. Minds

A mind's mood is read from what it remembers, needs and lives through;
strain is kept, building while mood stays low and eased by rest or relief.
Personality traits are discrete traits grown on the five-factor temperament,
seeded from it, inheritable and developed by what one does. Abilities are
what an entity can do; skills are practised pursuits that accumulate
experience; a technique is an ability with a skill precondition, shared
between those who hold the skill. The significant dead go to the planes after
life, and summoning them back is a costly possibility.

*Not yet built:* personality traits, techniques, the planes after life. Mood
is read live from needs, strain is kept as an account in played worlds, and
every entity holds its skills and five-factor disposition.

Links: draws on 62, 96, 158, 159, 160 · summary Mood is read from memory, needs and life, strain kept; personality traits grow on the five factors; abilities, skills, techniques; the planes after life.

### Schema 7. Culture and knowledge

A culture is read from its people and named when noted. Languages divide:
understanding needs a shared tongue, learned like a skill. Art and ritual are
crafts, and any craft can be politicized or ritualized. Technology is both the
world's generated tree and what minds and bearers know; an unheld technique
is lost and can be found again. Ages, the epochs of cultures, are the
record's (Record 6).

*Not yet built:* culture, languages, technology.

### Schema 8. One rules language; each world picks who leads

World rules and game rulesets are one binding language, at founding and at
play. Each world chooses whether the sim leads, rulesets calibrating to its
background (114), or its ruleset leads, the sim's background following it
wherever it specifies behaviour; the ledger, the record and the derivation
rule stay inviolable either way. A reading's bound and a competition's
definition sit in the world's rules. Effects entailed by a world's
composition, and a space scope, come later.

*Not yet built:* the per-world choice.

### Schema 9. Saves stay readable

Every save stays loadable: older versions remain readers, each checked by its
own hashes at every epoch checkpoint (Record 5), and saving again writes the
current version (v4). The flow record sits outside state: every matter move
when asked, each naming the process that moved it, handed over tick by tick;
the core keeps an observation hook. Sharing state (cohorts copied on write)
never changes serialisation or hashes.

### Schema 10. New rules are optional

New rules and fields are optional, absent meaning the old behaviour, so old
worlds keep their digests (the sun's parameters, a route's border, the map
family in founding). A world copies its defaults in explicitly, so growing a
default set never changes an old world. A feature gates operations, never
state, so every build reads one save format; the society layer sits behind
one, on by default and tested in its own lane.

Links: draws on 364, 397, 398, 886, 893, 979 · summary New rules are optional fields, absent meaning old behaviour; defaults are copied in explicitly; features gate operations, never state.

### Schema 11. Location and places

Places derive once at the base grain, clearance recorded on edges, so one
graph serves every body. The outdoors floods over walkable surface and splits
where cliffs, water or a world-rule climb (45 degrees by default) stop a
walker, a patch past the world's cap cut on the chunk grid (presets 16 to 512
base units a side, x by y allowed, 256 by default). An entity names its site
until its game places it, then its patch or room; places are the finest
location the sim holds.

Links: draws on 417, 418, 419, 420, 421, 422, 740 · summary Places derive once at base grain with clearance on edges; the outdoors splits by walkability under a capped patch size; an entity names its site until placed.

### Schema 12. The derivation rule

A world is a seed, its rules and the set of asserted facts; everything else is
derived on demand and may be discarded, and an entity is its seed plus the
history that touched it. The rule is also the cost model: storage follows
what was asserted, processing what is due, and nothing costs anything for
merely existing (Processes 18).

Links: draws on 14, 41, 69, 70 · summary A world is its seed, rules and asserted facts; all else is derived and discardable; storage follows the asserted, processing the due.

record; split from the four rules at Mark's word ("Split them").*

## Processes and rules


### Processes 1. The foreground resolves; the sim keeps count

Where a game resolves something in finer detail than the sim (a blow's
geometry), it hands the result back in the sim's terms (vigour drained, wounds
to parts). What comes back must pass the sim's invariants and agree with the
sim's own resolution in distribution.

Links: draws on 232 · summary A game's finer resolution hands back in the sim's terms, passing its invariants and agreeing in distribution.

("Move the handback principle up"). The rest of the area is not yet

### Processes 2. Property is explicit, by lots

Who can hold and own follows what a thing is: a critter gathers and holds by
its biology, with little capacity, and never owns; a sophont carries gear; a
pilot possesses a mech; any sophont, faction or polity can have possessions.
A critter has habitats, never a home it owns, though a named sapient can
assert one for it. Ownership is asserted, never inferred from holding, tenancy or making, and
names exact quantity lots; later matter never inherits earlier owners. Debits
select their lots (whole or an exact portion), with no FIFO or proportional
split; each operation declares which outputs keep the owners' claims or
closes them, and matter is conserved either way. A later process names a
produced lot by its operation, output and quantity. Work reserves its inputs
in a project ledger and spends from it; interruption keeps inputs and
progress, the work staying where it was made unless carried. The owners of
the reserved inputs together grant access; a polity grants by its authored
strategy (decision, unanimity or representatives); any eligible, authorized
subject may resume. A help action is a separate pathway, not yet designed.

Links: draws on 53, 54, 837, 838, 839, 840, 843, 844, 845, 846, 849, 850, 863 · summary Ownership is asserted by exact lots; debits select lots; projects reserve inputs; input owners grant access.

### Processes 3. Other genres adapt a ruleset

A tabletop ruleset belongs to its genre. A game of another genre keeps its
own mechanics and may take a ruleset as a selectable mode (Eponym's
progression, magic or costs), adapted to that genre rather than represented
faithfully (VTT 5). An adaptation is a named profile listing each departure
from the edition and the decision policy that stands in for each call a GM
would make, as a hybrid profile and an unattended world declare theirs. The
toolkit grown from the editions (checks, costs, effects, triggers) is shared
by every product.

Links: draws on 539, 540, 541, 543, vtt.5 · summary Another genre adapts a ruleset as a selectable mode, under a named profile of departures and stand-in decision policies; the toolkit is shared.

### Processes 4. Modifiers and conditions

Every modification applies effects from the world's one vocabulary; a glyph
is one source among traits, parts, items, places, conditions and relations.
Modifiers combine in layers the world orders, each reading only conditions
and earlier layers, each reading declaring its stacking (sum, highest,
lowest, product, replace); amounts are bounded expression trees, a default
set easy to group and calibrate. A modifier declares its scope (bearer, body,
relation, group, place, site; board adjacency is the game's) and whether it
applies to each member or to the group. The environment's conditions live on
places; a thing's own conditions are records beside its ledger (key,
magnitude, cause, optional end), one per key, a second application combining
by the key's stacking; a cohort tallies its members' causes, and a lift draws
one by seed.

*Not yet built:* condition records, a modifier's declared scope and
distribution, cause tallies. Bounded amounts and layered stacking (in
defence) are built.

Links: draws on 268, 427, 428, 429, 430, 431, 433, 435, 436 · summary One effect vocabulary, many sources; modifiers in world-ordered layers with declared stacking and scope; conditions on places or as records.

### Processes 5. The background and its shapes

Three layers always run in the background: an adapting ecology, a society
reforming by its inhabitants' goals and abilities, and polities, narratives
and arcs that change the world if left unattended, each taxonomy deep enough
for unpredictable results. Processes are one shape at every rung, choices
under scarcity, with two more: agentless field processes and rung transitions
(founding, splitting, incorporation, collapse). A game has handles on the
sim's verbs and its own on top. A graph derived from the bricks drives
processes at the rung above them. The definition widens only with fields the
probe proves; deep time is the same sim; nondeterministic water is fine where
a field directs it.

### Processes 6. Similitude and the crowd

Background and foreground agree; the background is cheaper by aggregation.
Anything of note runs as an individual; the fungible agree in distribution
over every threshold later processes read, within a bound in the world's rules
(0.2 per reading at first), certified beside a positive and a negative
control, an arm failing past 1% refused draws. What was watched is logged;
exact agreement is a world setting, statistical the default. The aggregate
runs a population model per site and lineage (counts by age band and
condition, its matter, births, deaths, meals, upkeep and moves as rates),
calling the individual runner's own meanings; an attended group runs its
noted members exactly; a parent and its unweaned young run as individuals.
The crowd's own chooser covers autonomous territories only. Lifting samples
individuals from an aggregate by seed, conditioned on every asserted fact;
restriction conserves the accounts back. A process is declarative enough for
its aggregate to derive from its one definition, so scripted hooks run only in
the foreground.

*Not yet built:* the population model (the execution plan's E4).

### Processes 7. Time

The clock counts a fine unit, a world setting, a minute by default; each
process runs on its own period in world time, a lineage's choices and moves on
its decision period, and anything finer is the foreground's. Bodily rates are
worked out when read, not acted each tick. The scheduler indexes processes by
the traits they need and keeps due events per member, a rate's crossings among
them, so idle time is skipped, and reconsiders a member only when what its
process reads has changed or a time gate ripens; an advance's limit and its
budget guard work, counting the members an evaluation stands for. Per-group
phases run in the foreground; the background keeps a shared grid. Epochs and
deep time are the record's (Record 7). Neighbouring worlds keep their own
clocks, synced at crossings. A pass's evaluations read its start and may run at
once, its commits serial in order; every draw is keyed by who acts, the tick
and the process, never by commit order, so equal best moves and every other
draw come out alike at any thread count. The sim is sharded by the place graph:
each shard an armillary actor with its own due-event queue, field passes
data-parallel inside a shard, and an ordered merge of cross-shard effects
designed before the shards, so one shard and N give the same state hash. On the
web the sim runs in one Worker, scaling down by shard count.

*Not yet built:* waking, parallel evaluation and order-free draws (the
execution plan's E1 to E3), rates worked out when read and decision periods
(E7, E8), shards, and worlds syncing at crossings.

### Processes 8. Competition

When two want what there isn't enough of, each side's methodology chooses
contest, yield, share or trade, and the sim resolves the choices; a lineage's
leaning is a trait. Competitions are keyed by what is contested; a member
enters every one it needs in a tick, each against the tick's start, costs
summed at its end. What cannot decide scrambles for shared ground, each taker
the same floored fraction from the pass's start, identity order deciding
nothing; what can decide competes, under shortage in pairwise encounters.

### Processes 9. Fights

Most contests (Processes 8) end at sizing up, by display or bluff; only a
close match escalates. Each round of a fight adds strain to both sides, plus
what the round costs them; a side whose strain passes its bearing breaks, a
break up advantaging it and a break down disadvantaging it, neither deciding
the fight. A fight ends when a side yields on re-sizing or is spent,
whichever comes first; the larger reserve does not simply win.

Links: draws on 116, 217, 221, 222, 223, processes.8 · summary Most contests end at sizing up; a fight builds strain against bearing; breaks shift advantage; it ends on yielding or being spent.

*Reviewed 2026-10-22, new: split from competition at Mark's word ("Fights
apart"); strain, bearing and yielding are built.*

### Processes 10. Breaking

Mood is read from needs (reserve, hunger, wounds as a share of cells), and
strain builds while it stays low (Schema 6). Past what it can bear, a mind
stops heeding, acts out, or rises, and the break leaves a mark on its traits.
Breaking does not spread: a riot is many minds breaking for the same reasons.
Whether a mind breaks down or up turns on its traits, the moment and a seeded
draw; what it can bear is set by its traits, its support and what it has been
through.

*Not yet built:* breaks, and support and history in bearing.

### Processes 11. Belief

How knowledge spreads and resolves is Processes 19. Any belief can be wrong (deceit, honest
mistake, a rumour retold), and entities act on what they believe; reach
carries versions of an event. Whether a belief takes turns on what the
receiver can check, who is telling, what it wants to hear and how it is told
(posing is a telling's manner); each entity's disposition shifts a baseline
drawn from the world entity's disposition. A secret is a taboo on telling, a
norm whose sanction is reputational, held as an explicit set of knowers' notes
rather than a field; its value is what release would do to its subject's
standing, its telling a choice by relation, opinion and goals, and it ends by
leaking into rumour.

*Not yet built:* false beliefs, the four-way weighing, secrets and taboos.
Knowing is notes plus reach, and tellings are built.

### Processes 12. Collective action

A faction acts by consent, peer to peer, and is never gated by disagreement:
its members fall back on the way of deciding their alignments rank highest,
and its general protocols belong to the world's ruleset. A polity adds a
constitution (Schema 5). A form of governance is who qualifies to decide and
by what act, data and never an enum, and the form a polity takes is chosen at
its founding by ranked preference over its members' alignments, weighted by
influence, ties on the seed. Where a polity vests ultimate authority in a
group, disagreement gates its acts, one act at a time. A constitution changes
by the polity's own way or its amendment rule; force outside it founds a new
polity and never takes over the old; secession is the seceders' own act.
Short of death a polity may be inactive (lacking support), suppressed (stopped
by another's enforcement), superseded (another binding its focus) or
subordinated (one faction's will running its methods), each read from the
record; it dies by its own dissolution or when nothing holds it any more.

*Not yet built:* secession, the four polity conditions, death when nothing
holds a polity. Constitutions are built behind the society feature.

### Processes 13. Making and learning

Value bears on scarcity, capability and material need, a reading at a place
and a time, never stored, and money is an asserted claim; need is derived from
alignment, acts and predilection. Taking an action is like crafting: an act's
score is need times trust times approval, every factor derived, and what gets
made follows the same score; a fungible act folds into its cohort and leaves
no note, so grinding yields competence and never a legend, and a fey mood is a
triggered need. In aggregate, exchange is flow along routes from cheap to
dear. A skill rises by doing, teaching, study and breakthroughs and decays
slowly to a floor (Schema 6). Techniques are found in the canon and invented
by masters, invention driven by need, contact, temperament and mastery. An
item improves as far as its maker's skill and its story allow, never capped
by its material.

*Not yet built:* value, fey moods, skill decay, techniques, invention, item
improvement. Skills are held on every entity.

### Processes 15. Matter moves by declared processes

Matter is conserved through every process (Ecology 6). A dev's placement
comes from a dev source outside the conserved total, into any declared
account, its issue and the assisted label living in the history. Declared
conversions (synthesis, digestion, mineralization) are checked; a synthesis
yields the actor's own lineage's matter, so whatever synthesizes is a
producer. Each consumer lineage has one feeding process, eating across its
prey's matter once a period, prey drawn by holdings; a process declaring port
feeding targets what its port admits. Native declares a terrain operation and
the game resolves its geometry, the same session admitting the edit; earth a
carver can't hold is heaped beside the cut.

### Processes 16. Journeys

A migration departs and arrives after the route's travel time, its next route
only after arrival. On the way, age and upkeep continue, the soil they make
carried to the next site; a child born en route shares the rest of the
journey; an event en route is located on the journey, with no site reach
until a later telling; a corpse finishes the journey and becomes available at
its end.

### Processes 17. Magic

## Ecology


### Ecology 1. Two doors change a lineage

Reproduction rerolls expression, a lottery set by the heterogeneity of its
genes; the epoch boundary changes the lineage itself, like the shop between
rounds, folding in the variants that prospered (tracts and systems). Every
lineage, played or not, adapts, inherits and develops there, weighing its
adaptation against the rest of the web by growing a copy forward. Discovery is
the lessons learned from eating a part whole. Lineages take their boundary

living members. A player may branch or fork a line, with a plan to compete or
cooperate with its origin.

## Record and history

### Record 1. Consistency is chosen per domain

refusing it.

## Mesocosm

### Mesocosm 1. What the player plays

The player plays a critter of its lineage, and how the rest of the lineage
relates depends on its traits: competitors, a cohort, or extensions of one
organism (a fungal monocreature, a swarm). Mesocosm is directed, never driven
(Directing 3). At a birth the player keeps the parent by default and may take
the offspring, reversibly. Speciating (splitting the line and naming it; "the
name is the doing") is the player's act; expressing a candidate is the
critter's own development.

Links: draws on 155, 175, 183, 202, 682 · summary The player plays a critter whose lineage relates by its traits; keeps the parent at a birth; speciating is the player's act.

*Reviewed 2026-10-22, new. Mark: "Both as drafted".*

### Mesocosm 2. The only directive is a nudge

A click draws the critter's attention to a place (a place-graph handle) or a
thing, which it answers by its own lights, weighed by its bond; a right click
shows a ring of its possible acts toward the target. No gesture warns:
avoidance comes from experience and stance. Priorities, ranges, homes and
stances grow from where attention keeps leading. The bond grows from how well
the nudges served the critter; whether it carries to the next generation is a
world setting (fresh, seeded, whole), seeded by default.

Links: draws on 176, 177, 178, 205, 214, 215, 216, 228 · summary The only directive is a nudge by click; standing orders grow from attention; the bond carries by world setting.

*Reviewed 2026-10-22, new. Mark: "Both as drafted".*

### Mesocosm 3. Starting, losing and going on

A player starts when they choose: at the start of the world's habitability for
their critter (the first epoch boundary where a site meets the lineage's
conditions and a member lives there), or with as much added time as they like.
Losing is not the end: after a regional collapse members migrate along routes
and the player may start again elsewhere; when a lineage ends the player picks
another here or a new world, their call. A total collapse ends the world as a
game, still watchable.
